How much can it take?

A living thing is partly defined by what it recovers from. So the disturbance laboratory (Lane 4) and the replication lane (Lane 3) wounded Orbium, carefully and reproducibly, more than two thousand times between them. They dimmed the whole body, cut a hole in its centre, sliced its flank, or added material in front of it, then waited to see whether it recovered.

Every single run either came back to the normal Orbium or faded to nothing; even the runs closest to the edge collapsed within 3 to 8 time units. None turned into something else. For each kind of wound there is one sharp threshold between the two.

S001 has a single, sharp, all-or-nothing survival edge for each of four standard disturbances {{claim:C023}}, confirmed by Lane 3's separate implementation of the whole protocol.

Where matters more than how much

The surprise is the position of the edges. Orbium shrugs off losing a tenth of its mass when the loss is spread evenly. Take about half as much from its centre and it dies.

WoundLethal at about
Central hole−5% of mass
Slice from one flank−8 to −9%
Uniform dimming−10%
Material added in front+28%

Where mass is removed matters more than how much {{claim:C026}}. Total mass after the wound does not predict survival across wound types at all {{claim:C028}}. Near the dimming threshold, doomed runs linger before collapsing, and the delay grows the closer they start to the edge, as if passing near an unstable balance point {{claim:C029}}.

Where the numbers bend The thresholds are brackets measured on one grid at one timestep, not constants of nature. Quarter the timestep and the flank-slice and frontal-addition edges move by 11 to 13% {{claim:C027}}: the hatched rows in panel d. The central hole cannot be measured more finely at R = 13, because three missing cells survive and four do not. And the five wound timings used are consecutive steps, a thin sample of the creature's cycle; Lane 6 found one later timing at which a 10% dimming kills.

Room 4 returns to wounds with a different animal, a bound pair, where the same flank slice has a very different outcome.
